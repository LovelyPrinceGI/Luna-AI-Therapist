\chapter{RESULT}

We conducted two automated evaluation experiments using GPT-4o as a zero-shot judge across CBT therapy dialogues generated under four distinct therapist configurations. \textbf{Experiment 1 (RQ1)} evaluates the Dissonance-Aware framework's advantage over three baseline conditions on GPT-4o mini across 100 dialogues (400 evaluation sessions). \textbf{Experiment 2 (RQ2)} replicates the full 4-condition experiment across four LLM backbones, GPT-4o mini, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct, and DeepSeek V3.2, on 100 dialogues, yielding 1,600 evaluation sessions. All conditions share the same dataset, client persona, evaluation rubric, and GPT-4o judge.

The four therapist configurations are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline:} Text-only prompting without any emotional metadata.
    \item \textbf{Emotion (Text-Only):} The therapist receives the client's text along with text-derived Valence-Arousal (VA) values.
    \item \textbf{Multimodal (Vocal-Aware):} The therapist receives both text-derived VA and speech-derived VA from the client's vocal cues, but without explicit dissonance computation.
    \item \textbf{Dissonance-Aware:} The therapist receives text VA, speech VA, and the computed delta between modalities (emotional dissonance signal), enabling the model to detect and respond to mismatches between what the client says and how they sound.
\end{itemize}

GPT-4o scored each full dialogue session on seven quantitative metrics spanning three evaluation dimensions: \textbf{Therapist Skills} (Understanding, Interpersonal Effectiveness), \textbf{Client Alliance} (Affective Bond), and \textbf{Cognitive Therapy Rating Scale} (Collaboration, Guided Discovery, Focus, Strategy). Scores were averaged across all 100 dialogues for both experiments. Table \ref{tab:evaluation_results} summarizes the RQ1 results.

\textbf{Key findings:} The Dissonance-Aware method outperformed both text-only baselines (Baseline and Emotion) on all 7 evaluation metrics, with gains of +0.53 to +0.80 over Baseline (e.g., Understanding 4.70 vs.\ 4.17, CTRS Collaboration 4.06 vs.\ 3.26). Against Multimodal (Vocal-Aware), the two vocal conditions were near-equivalent (differences $\leq 0.10$ on six of seven metrics), with Dissonance-Aware leading on CTRS Focus (3.64 vs.\ 3.48, +0.16). The Multimodal method itself improved over Emotion (Text-Only) on Understanding (4.76 vs.\ 4.44), demonstrating that vocal cues contribute measurable value beyond text-level emotional information, while the explicit dissonance computation adds focused value on session directionality.

\section{Result Corresponding to RQ1: Dissonance Advantage}

RQ1 asks whether the Dissonance-Aware therapist framework, which computes emotional mismatch (VA delta) between text and speech, consistently outperforms the three baseline conditions. Table~\ref{tab:evaluation_results} presents the average scores across 100 dialogues for each condition.

Dissonance-Aware outperformed Baseline on all 7 metrics: Understanding rose from 4.17 to 4.70 (+0.53), CTRS Collaboration from 3.26 to 4.06 (+0.80), CTRS Guided Discovery from 2.48 to 3.16 (+0.68), CTRS Focus from 3.05 to 3.64 (+0.59), and CTRS Strategy from 2.45 to 3.07 (+0.62). It likewise outperformed Emotion (Text-Only) on all 7 metrics. Against Multimodal (Vocal-Aware), the two vocal conditions were near-equivalent (differences $\leq 0.10$ on six of seven metrics), with Dissonance-Aware leading most clearly on CTRS Focus (3.64 vs.\ 3.48). This indicates that, with stateful therapist context, the addition of vocal information drives the majority of the gain over text-only baselines, while the explicit dissonance computation contributes additional, focused improvement on maintaining session direction.

These findings support H1 with respect to the text-only baselines: both vocal conditions improve therapeutic attunement beyond what text-level emotional information alone provides, with Dissonance-Aware adding its clearest incremental value on CTRS Focus.

\subsection{Statistical Significance and the Role of the Dissonance Flag}
\label{subsec:significance}

Table~\ref{tab:significance} reports paired $t$-tests over the 100 per-dialogue total scores (sum of the seven metrics) for GPT-4o mini. Every step of the information ladder is significant except the last: adding text VA (Emotion) improves over Baseline ($t = 4.21$), adding vocal metadata (Multimodal) improves over Emotion ($t = 4.81$), and Dissonance-Aware is statistically indistinguishable from Multimodal ($t = -0.39$; 43 wins, 13 ties, 44 losses). Per-metric tests show the same pattern: Dissonance-Aware differs from Baseline on all seven metrics ($t \geq 6.5$), while its largest difference from Multimodal is on CTRS Focus (+0.16, $t = 1.74$), a marginal trend rather than a significant gap. Equivalence with Multimodal does not violate H1, which predicted the highest \emph{or tied-highest} scores: the dissonance condition is never significantly worse than any condition on any metric.

Why are Dissonance-Aware and Multimodal equivalent on this backbone? The dissonance flag fired on only 5 of 1,000 turns (0.5\%): because the Zonos director assigns vocal emotion from the text content, speech VA tracks text VA closely, and natural text--voice mismatch almost never occurs in synthetic audio. On 99.5\% of turns the Dissonance-Aware prompt therefore receives the same information as the Multimodal prompt, and the vocal metadata itself, not the flag, drives the large significant gain over the text-only baselines. The scripted demonstration with a real human voice (Section~\ref{sec:chat-demo}) shows the converse: when a genuine mismatch is present ($\delta_V = -1.05$), the flag fires and the therapist responds with targeted exploration. The dissonance mechanism is thus functional but rarely triggered by synthetic voices; controlled manipulation of text--voice mismatch, synthesizing voices that deliberately contradict the text, is the natural next experiment to test the flag's causal effect directly.

The same pattern holds across backbones (Table~\ref{tab:flagrate-backbone}). The Dissonance--Multimodal difference is significant only on Qwen-2.5-72B ($t = 2.55$); on DeepSeek and Claude it is a positive but non-significant trend, and on GPT-4o mini the two conditions are equivalent. The magnitude of the difference tracks the dissonance flag rate: GPT-4o mini's synthetic dialogues trigger the flag on only 0.5\% of turns, whereas the other backbones' dialogues trigger it on 1.0--4.7\% of turns, giving the dissonance prompt more opportunities to act. Claude is the exception that clarifies the rule: although its dialogues have the highest flag rate (4.7\%), its near-ceiling Baseline leaves little headroom, so the added signal translates into only a small gain.

To quantify this overhead, we measured per-component inference times across 100 dialogues generated under the Baseline and Dissonance-Aware conditions using Claude Sonnet 4.6 as a representative backbone. The Dissonance-Aware pipeline adds \textbf{21.92 seconds per dialogue} (+29\%) relative to the Baseline, an increase from 75.38s to 97.33s. Three components account for the majority of this overhead:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Therapist LLM (+1.64s/response).} The single largest contributor. Dissonance-Aware prompts are richer: they include VA values, vocal descriptors, and the dissonance delta flagged for therapeutic exploration, which elicits longer, more deliberative therapist responses. The increased token generation is inherent to the deeper reflection the framework is designed to encourage.
    \item \textbf{WavLM speech emotion extraction (+0.25s/utterance).} Each client audio waveform is processed through a WavLM-based SER model to predict valence and arousal from the acoustic signal. This step is absent in the Baseline condition, where only vad-bert (text-side) runs.
    \item \textbf{Librosa vocal descriptor extraction (+0.09s/utterance).} Pitch, loudness, and speaking rate are computed from the raw audio using signal processing. While individually negligible, this step must run for every utterance.
\end{enumerate}

The Dissonance Computation itself is instantaneous ($< 0.01$s): it is a simple arithmetic comparison of scalar VA values. VadBERT runs in both conditions with near-identical timing.

\textbf{Cost--Benefit Tradeoff.} For GPT-4o mini, where the Dissonance framework yields a +0.64 average uplift and narrows the gap to Claude's Baseline performance, the 29\% time increase represents a favorable tradeoff: a modest computational premium for a substantial therapeutic quality gain. For Claude, where the uplift is marginal (+0.11), the overhead may be harder to justify on efficiency grounds alone, though the framework's value in this case may lie less in quantitative improvement and more in the qualitative shift toward exploring hidden affect at moments of detected dissonance. The practical implication is that the Dissonance-Aware framework is most cost-effective when paired with smaller, lower-cost models that have the most room to benefit from additional emotional reasoning.
