\begin{center}
    \large{\bf ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

    The rising demand for mental health support has accelerated the development of AI-driven psychological counseling, yet most current systems rely solely on textual information and cannot perceive the gap between what a client says and how they sound. This thesis introduces a multimodal Dissonance-Aware framework for CBT-oriented therapist response generation: text-derived and speech-derived Valence-Arousal representations are extracted in parallel (vad-bert and WavLM), their per-dimension delta is computed as an emotional dissonance signal, and the signal --- together with natural-language prosodic descriptors --- is injected into the therapist LLM's prompt with instructions to explore mismatch gently rather than accuse. Four therapist conditions (Baseline, Emotion text-only VA, Multimodal vocal-aware, Dissonance-Aware) were compared across four LLM backbones (GPT-4o mini, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct, DeepSeek V3.2) on 100 shared-problem-seed dialogues per cell (1,600 sessions), evaluated by a three-layer blind protocol: absolute GPT-4o grading on seven MIRROR/CTRS-derived metrics, per-seed paired statistics, and 800 swap-consistent pairwise preference comparisons with a cross-family second judge. Dissonance-Aware achieved the highest score on all seven metrics on GPT-4o mini, with significant gains over Baseline on the CTRS craft dimensions (Guided Discovery, Focus, Strategy; pairwise net win-rate 0.680, $p = 0.0001$) and a significant advantage on DeepSeek (pairwise $p = 0.00004$) invisible to absolute scores at ceiling. Crucially, the Multimodal condition --- identical vocal inputs without dissonance framing --- regressed below the text-only Baseline on both weaker backbones, while Dissonance-Aware defeated it decisively (net win-rate 0.800, $p < 10^{-4}$): the dissonance computation, not the vocal data itself, is the active ingredient. The benefit is headroom-gated (neutral on Qwen, unmeasurable at Claude's ceiling, with no significant loss on any backbone), making the framework most valuable exactly where deployment is most cost-constrained. Limitations include synthetic English-only dialogue, alpha-stage TTS prosody, and fair-but-directionally-consistent inter-judge agreement ($\kappa = 0.217$); human expert validation is the primary next step.
